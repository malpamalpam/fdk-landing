You've landed a job with a language school in Warsaw or a teaching platform, and then comes the email: "Please send us a VAT invoice." You're not Polish, you don't have a company, and setting one up (JDG) means ZUS contributions of roughly 1,900 PLN a month, an accountant, and paperwork in a language you're still learning. The good news: you can invoice a language school in Poland without a company. This guide explains how, using a business incubator.

## Why schools insist on invoices

Polish schools have two legal ways to pay a non-employee teacher: a civil-law contract (umowa zlecenia / umowa o dzieło) that they administer themselves, or an invoice from a business. Many schools prefer invoices because contracts make them responsible for social security (ZUS) registration and tax withholding. So they say "send an invoice" and leave the problem with you.

## Your three options

| Option | Monthly cost | Paperwork | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Register a sole proprietorship (JDG) | ZUS from ~0 (start relief, 6 months) to ~1,900 PLN; accountant 150–300 PLN | high; Polish-language filings | high, stable income (15k+ PLN) |
| Ask the school for a direct contract | none | medium; school must agree | one school, long-term |
| **Business incubator** | 400 PLN/month flat fee | low; one agreement, then online | most freelance teachers |

A business incubator (inkubator przedsiębiorczości) is a foundation that lends you its legal entity. It signs a contract with you, invoices the school in its own name, receives the payment, deducts tax and commission, and pays the rest to you.

## Step by step: first invoice in one week

1. **Apply online** – a short form with your details, residence status, and what you teach.
2. **Sign the incubator agreement** – electronically; you'll need an ID or passport and your residence document (card, visa, UKR status).
3. **Give the school the incubator's details** – company name, address, NIP (tax ID). The school signs a short service agreement with the incubator or just accepts invoices.
4. **Teach.** Keep a simple log of hours or lessons.
5. **Report the invoice** – at month end you submit: school name, amount, period. The incubator issues the invoice within one business day.
6. **School pays the incubator**, the incubator signs a contract of mandate (umowa zlecenia) with you for that month and pays you out.

## What kind of contract you'll be on

Teaching lessons is a "service of diligent performance", so the correct contract is a **contract of mandate (umowa zlecenia)**. It comes with social and health insurance contributions – which is actually useful: it gives you **public health insurance (NFZ)**, and for residence-permit applications it counts as legal, documented income. Students under 26 pay no contributions and no income tax up to 85,528 PLN a year.

If you also create your own materials (a course, worksheets, recordings), that part can be a **contract for specific work (umowa o dzieło)** with 50% deductible costs – effectively 6% tax, no contributions. More on that in [taxes for online English teachers in Poland](/blog/online-english-teacher-taxes-poland).

## What you take home – example

Teacher, 29, non-student, no other employment in Poland, invoicing a school 5,000 PLN net per month:

- Contract of mandate: social contributions ~686 PLN, health ~388 PLN, income tax ~370 PLN → ~3,550 PLN, minus incubator fee of 400 PLN/month.
- Same teacher with 60% of the amount as materials (specific work, 50% costs) and 40% lessons (mandate): roughly 4,150 PLN minus fee.
- Same amount via your own JDG with "start relief": tax + health contribution ~315 PLN + accountant ~200 PLN → ~4,000 PLN, but with full personal liability and Polish-language bookkeeping.

## Multiple schools and platforms

One incubator agreement covers any number of clients: two schools, Preply, a private student who wants a receipt. Each gets its own invoice; you get one monthly settlement. Foreign platforms (Preply, italki, Cambly) are invoiced in EUR/USD without Polish VAT.

## Questions to ask any incubator before you sign

- Is the foundation a registered VAT payer with an EU VAT number?
- What is the commission, and is there a minimum monthly fee if you earn nothing that month?
- How many days after the school pays do you receive your money?
- Will they issue an income certificate for a residence-permit application?
- Do they communicate in English?
