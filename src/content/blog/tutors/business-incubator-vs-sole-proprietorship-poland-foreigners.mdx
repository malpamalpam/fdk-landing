"Should I register a JDG or use an incubator?" This is the most common question we hear from foreigners working in Poland – teachers, translators, IT freelancers, designers, copywriters. The answer depends on how much you earn, how long you plan to stay, and how much Polish bureaucracy you're willing to handle. This guide compares the two options side by side so you can make the right call for your situation.

## What each option actually is

**JDG (jednoosobowa działalność gospodarcza)** is a sole proprietorship – the standard way to run a one-person business in Poland. You register in CEIDG, get a NIP and REGON, choose a tax form, register for VAT (or claim an exemption), and start issuing invoices under your own name. You are personally liable for everything.

**A business incubator (inkubator przedsiębiorczości)** is a foundation that lets you work through its legal entity. You sign a civil-law contract (umowa zlecenia or umowa o dzieło) with the incubator, it invoices your clients, receives payment, deducts tax and its commission, and pays you. You are not a business owner – you are a contractor. For more on how the invoicing works in practice, see [how to invoice a language school without a company](/blog/invoice-language-school-poland-without-company).

## Cost comparison at four income levels

The numbers below assume: age 30+, no other employment in Poland, JDG on 12% flat tax (podatek liniowy) after the 6-month "start relief" period for ZUS, incubator fee of 400 PLN/month, and work that qualifies for umowa o dzieło with 50% copyright costs (translations, materials creation) or umowa zlecenia (teaching, consulting).

### 3,000 PLN/month (low income)

| | Incubator (umowa o dzieło, 50% costs) | JDG (flat tax) |
|---|---|---|
| Gross income | 3,000 | 3,000 |
| Income tax | 180 | 360 |
| ZUS + health | 0 | ~2,250 |
| Accountant | 0 | ~200 |
| Incubator fee | 400 | – |
| **Net payout** | **~2,420** | **~190** |

At this income level, JDG is not viable. ZUS alone eats almost the entire income. The incubator wins by over 2,000 PLN.

### 6,000 PLN/month (moderate income)

| | Incubator (umowa o dzieło, 50% costs) | JDG (flat tax) |
|---|---|---|
| Gross income | 6,000 | 6,000 |
| Income tax | 360 | 720 |
| ZUS + health | 0 | ~2,250 |
| Accountant | 0 | ~200 |
| Incubator fee | 400 | – |
| **Net payout** | **~5,240** | **~2,830** |

Still a clear win for the incubator. The gap is roughly 2,400 PLN per month.

### 10,000 PLN/month (good income)

| | Incubator (umowa o dzieło, 50% costs) | JDG (flat tax) |
|---|---|---|
| Gross income | 10,000 | 10,000 |
| Income tax | 600 | 1,200 |
| ZUS + health | 0 | ~2,250 |
| Accountant | 0 | ~200 |
| Incubator fee | 400 | – |
| **Net payout** | **~9,000** | **~6,350** |

The incubator still leads by about 2,650 PLN. At this level, JDG starts to make sense only if you have significant business expenses to deduct (equipment, software, travel) or need to build a business brand.

### 20,000 PLN/month (high income)

| | Incubator (umowa o dzieło, 50% costs) | JDG (flat tax) |
|---|---|---|
| Gross income | 20,000 | 20,000 |
| Income tax | 1,200 | 2,400 |
| ZUS + health | 0 | ~2,250 |
| Accountant | 0 | ~250 |
| Incubator fee | 400 | – |
| **Net payout** | **~18,400** | **~15,100** |

Even at 20,000 PLN the incubator is ahead, though the percentage gap narrows. The break-even point shifts if the 50% costs cap (120,000 PLN/year) is reached – at that point, additional income is taxed at 12% without the deduction, and the JDG's flat tax becomes relatively more competitive.

<Note>
If your work does NOT qualify for 50% copyright costs (e.g., pure consulting, manual labour, administrative services), the incubator uses umowa zlecenia, which carries ZUS contributions. In that case, the cost advantage shrinks and JDG may win at lower income levels. Ask us which contract type applies to your work.
</Note>

## ZUS: the biggest difference

**JDG:** You must pay ZUS regardless of income. After the initial relief periods (6 months of "start relief" at ~380 PLN, then 24 months of "small ZUS+" at reduced rates), the full contribution is roughly **1,900 PLN/month** in 2026. If you earn nothing one month, you still pay. If you earn 3,000 PLN, you still pay 1,900 PLN.

**Incubator:** On umowa o dzieło, ZUS is **zero**. On umowa zlecenia, contributions are proportional to your income (~21% for social + ~9% for health), but there is no minimum. Earn nothing, pay nothing.

This fixed-cost nature of JDG ZUS is what makes it devastating at low and irregular income levels – exactly the situation most freelancers face in their first year in Poland.

## Paperwork and language

**JDG:** Everything is in Polish. CEIDG registration, ZUS declarations (ZUS DRA, ZUS RCA), tax filings (PIT-36L or PIT-36), VAT returns (JPK_V7), revenue ledgers. You'll need a Polish-speaking accountant (150–300 PLN/month), and even then you're signing documents you may not fully understand.

**Incubator:** One agreement in English (or bilingual). Monthly reporting is a short online form: who you worked for, how much, what period. The incubator handles all filings, all declarations, all communication with ZUS and the tax office. You receive a PIT-11 at the end of the year for your annual tax return – which many incubators also help you file.

For foreigners who don't speak Polish fluently, this is not a minor convenience – it is the difference between understanding your tax situation and blindly trusting an accountant.

## Health insurance

**JDG:** You get NFZ public health insurance through your ZUS contributions. It's part of the package you're already paying for.

**Incubator on umowa zlecenia:** You also get NFZ, because the mandate contract includes health insurance contributions. This is the standard contract for teaching, tutoring, and consulting.

**Incubator on umowa o dzieło:** No health insurance contributions. You'll need to arrange coverage independently – either voluntary NFZ enrollment (~500 PLN/month), private insurance, or coverage through a spouse or university.

If health insurance is important to you (and it should be), make sure your incubator contract type includes it, or budget for private cover.

## Residence permits

Both JDG and the incubator produce documentation that voivodeship offices accept for [temporary residence permit applications](/blog/business-incubator-poland-residence-permit):

- **JDG:** PIT returns, CEIDG registration, revenue ledger, bank statements.
- **Incubator:** Contracts (umowa zlecenia/o dzieło), PIT-11, bank statements, certificate from the incubator confirming cooperation.

We have helped dozens of members use incubator documentation for successful residence-permit applications in Warsaw, Krakow, Wroclaw, and other cities. The key is showing regular, documented, legal income – and the incubator provides exactly that.

## When to switch from incubator to JDG

Consider registering a JDG when:

- **Your income is consistently above 15,000 PLN/month** and your work doesn't qualify for 50% copyright costs.
- **You have significant deductible expenses** – a home office, equipment, software subscriptions, travel – that reduce your JDG tax base.
- **You want to build a brand** – your own company name, website, logo on invoices.
- **You need to hire employees or subcontractors** under your own entity.
- **You're applying for a mortgage** – some banks treat JDG income more favourably than civil-law contracts.

The switch is not a dramatic event. Many of our members start with the incubator, work for 6–18 months, stabilise their income and client base, and then register a JDG when it makes financial sense. There is no lock-in period.

## Who should NOT use an incubator

An incubator is not the right fit if you:

- **Sell physical products** – incubators are designed for services, not e-commerce or retail.
- **Need to sign long-term commercial contracts** in your own company name (leases, distribution agreements).
- **Want to register as a VAT payer yourself** to reclaim input VAT on large purchases.
- **Earn over 15,000 PLN/month consistently** on work that doesn't qualify for 50% costs – at that level, JDG's fixed ZUS becomes a smaller percentage of income and the flat tax may be more efficient.

## The bottom line

For most foreigners freelancing in Poland – especially in the first one to three years – the incubator is cheaper, simpler, and less risky than a JDG. You avoid the fixed ZUS burden, skip the Polish-language paperwork, and keep more of what you earn. As your situation evolves, switching to a JDG is straightforward.
