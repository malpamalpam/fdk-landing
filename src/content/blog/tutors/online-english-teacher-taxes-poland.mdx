Teaching English online from Poland is one of the most common ways foreigners earn here – and one of the most common sources of tax confusion. "The platform is in the US, so do I even pay in Poland?" "What is ZUS and do I need it?" "Someone told me about 50% costs – is that real?" Here is the 2026 picture in plain language, plus how a business incubator turns all of it into one monthly settlement.

## First question: are you a Polish tax resident?

If you live in Poland more than 183 days in a year, or your home and family are here, you are a Polish tax resident and pay Polish income tax on **worldwide** income – Preply in the US, a school in Spain, a private student in Japan. The platform's country doesn't matter; your tax residence does.

If you're not sure, ask yourself: where do I sleep most nights, where is my bank account, where is my partner? Those are the factors the tax office looks at.

## Two types of contract, two tax outcomes

In a business incubator, your income goes through one of two civil-law contracts:

### Contract of mandate (umowa zlecenia)

This is the standard for **teaching lessons** – live, online, in a school, on a platform.

- **ZUS contributions**: social (~13.7%) + health (9%) are deducted from your pay. This gives you public health insurance and counts towards your pension. Students under 26: no ZUS at all.
- **Income tax**: 12% after 20% deductible costs. Under 26: 0% up to 85,528 PLN/year (the "youth relief").
- **Example**: 5,000 PLN gross → after ZUS and tax → ~3,550 PLN net (over 26, non-student). Under 26 student: 5,000 PLN net.

### Contract for specific work (umowa o dzieło)

This is for **creating something** and transferring the copyright: a course, a set of worksheets, a video series, a textbook, exam prep materials.

- **No ZUS** – no contributions, no health insurance from this contract.
- **50% author's costs** – if you transfer copyright, your taxable base is half the gross amount. Tax = 12% × 50% = effectively **6%**.
- **Example**: 5,000 PLN gross → costs 2,500 PLN → tax 300 PLN → 4,700 PLN net.

<Note>You cannot call regular lessons a "dzieło" – the tax office will reclassify it and charge back-contributions. Only genuine creative output with copyright transfer qualifies.</Note>

## Can you combine both?

Yes, and this is the smartest approach for many teachers. If you teach 20 hours a month (mandate) and also create a 10-lesson course (dzieło), split the income accordingly. The incubator prepares both contracts and handles the split in one monthly settlement.

## What about VAT?

Language teaching is generally **VAT-exempt** in Poland under art. 43 ust. 1 pkt 28 of the VAT Act (private tutoring by a teacher). The incubator applies the correct VAT treatment per invoice. If you teach through a school or platform, the exemption may apply differently – the incubator checks this for each client.

For foreign platforms (Preply, italki, Cambly) based outside Poland, invoices are issued without Polish VAT (reverse charge or "not subject to tax" for non-EU companies).

## Platforms: Preply, italki, Cambly, Tutlo

Each platform handles payments differently:

- **Preply**: pays to your bank account or PayPal; you can redirect payments to the incubator's account and have them invoiced.
- **italki**: similar to Preply; withdrawals go to your bank, then you settle with the incubator.
- **Cambly**: pays in USD via PayPal; the incubator accepts PayPal transfers.
- **Tutlo** (Polish platform): usually works on a B2B contract; the incubator signs the contract and invoices Tutlo directly.

The key: the incubator needs to know the platform and payment flow **before** you start. Set it up once, then it's automatic each month.

## Annual tax return

The incubator issues a **PIT-11** form by the end of February, listing your income from mandates and dzieło contracts. You file your annual PIT-37 (or let the tax office pre-fill it in Twój e-PIT). If you only earned through the incubator, filing takes about 10 minutes.

## When the incubator makes sense – and when it doesn't

**Use the incubator if:**
- You teach a few to 20+ hours a week and earn up to ~15,000 PLN/month
- You have multiple clients (schools, platforms, private students)
- You don't want to deal with Polish bookkeeping
- You need health insurance from your work (mandate gives you NFZ)
- You're a foreigner and want documented income for your residence permit

**Consider JDG (sole trader) if:**
- You earn 15,000+ PLN/month consistently
- You qualify for the 6-month ZUS start relief
- You're comfortable with Polish-language tax filings (or hiring an accountant)
